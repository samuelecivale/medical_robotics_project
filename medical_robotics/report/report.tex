\begin{abstract}
This report presents a Unity-based simulation of a ROSA-like six-degree-of-freedom surgical robot implementing double Remote-Center-of-Motion (RCM) control, following the task-priority formulation of Aghakhani et al.\ (ICRA 2013). A from-scratch damped-least-squares controller with a null-space secondary objective handles four operating modes and a three-phase safe-insertion sequence. A Task~2 failure caused by wrist-joint saturation was resolved with strict tip-first hierarchy, an active-set constraint on joint~4, adaptive damping, and bounded integration substeps. The updated controller is evaluated on a 3361-sample Unity log using clinically stricter thresholds of 2\,mm for entry-RCM stability and 3\,mm for tip-target accuracy. All nine automatic validation checks pass, including zero skull/corridor violation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Clinical Motivation}
% =====================================================================

Stereotactic neurosurgical procedures --- biopsy, electrode placement, catheter insertion --- require a rigid tool to reach a deep target through a single, pre-planned skull opening. Once the entry point is fixed, any lateral force exerted by the tool shaft against the edges of that opening risks tearing or bruising healthy tissue along the corridor. Robotic assistance can enforce this constraint far more precisely than a hand-guided or passively braced instrument: the robot's controller can be made to guarantee that the tool axis always intersects the entry point, regardless of how the arm reconfigures to reach different targets.

The mechanism that formalizes this guarantee is the \emph{Remote Center of Motion} (RCM): a virtual pivot point, external to the robot's own mechanical structure, through which the tool shaft is constrained to pass at all times. Unlike a mechanical RCM (a physical gimbal, as used in some laparoscopic robots), the RCM studied here is purely a \emph{control} objective, implemented as an additional task in a task-priority kinematic controller.

This project implements and validates such a controller in Unity, for a ROSA-like six-degree-of-freedom manipulator, following the double-RCM task formulation of Aghakhani et al.\ (ICRA 2013), in which the RCM constraint can be located either at the entry point or, in an alternate mode, effectively relocated to the target itself.

\section{The Remote-Center-of-Motion Concept}
% =====================================================================

The RCM point is expressed as a point sliding along the tool shaft:
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{RCM}}(q, \lambda) = p_{\mathrm{base}}(q) + \lambda \, L \, z(q),
\end{equation}
where $p_{\mathrm{base}}(q)$ is the position of the tool's proximal reference point (a function of the joint configuration $q$), $z(q)$ is the tool's axial unit vector, $L$ is the tool length, and $\lambda \in [0.02, 0.995]$ is a dimensionless \emph{penetration coordinate} that slides the candidate RCM point along the shaft as insertion proceeds. The controller drives $\lambda$ together with the joint velocities so that $p_{\mathrm{RCM}}(q,\lambda)$ coincides with the desired entry (or target) marker, without requiring any physical hinge at that location.

Enforcing this constraint changes the character of the motion completely. Without it, nothing prevents the shaft from levering sideways against the entry point as the arm reconfigures to track a moving target. With the RCM task stacked alongside the tip task, the controller can move the tip to a new target while keeping the pivot locked at the entry, or --- in the alternate task modes studied here --- relocate the effective pivot to the target while the entry side sweeps a bounded cone. This is the ``double RCM'' idea referenced in the title: the same controller structure realizes an RCM at the entry \emph{or} at the target, selected by which task mode is active.

\subsection{Task 3: The Safe-Insertion Sequence}
\label{sec:task3}

Task 3 is a three-phase finite-state machine that mirrors the intended clinical workflow (a fourth internal state, \texttt{Done}, marks completion):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{ApproachEntry} --- the needle axis is aligned with the entry--target line while the tip approaches from outside the trocar; the RCM constraint is \emph{not yet} active.
\item \textbf{PierceEntry} --- the tip travels from the outside approach point to the entry (trocar) point.
\item \textbf{InsertToTarget} --- the RCM is locked at the entry point and the tip advances along the shaft axis to the target, terminating in the \texttt{Done} state once both the insertion progress and the tip-to-target distance fall below fixed thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Controller Architecture}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Task-Priority Formulation}

At every control step, the active point tasks (tip position, entry/target cone tracking, and the RCM point) are stacked into a single differential kinematics problem:
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{x}_{\mathrm{task}} \\ \dot{x}_{\mathrm{RCM}} \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} J_{\mathrm{task}} & 0 \\ J_{\mathrm{RCM}} \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{q} \\ \dot{\lambda} \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
where each row block is itself the vertical stack of whichever point tasks are active in the current mode. Each task's desired velocity is a saturated proportional term on its Cartesian error,
\begin{equation}
\dot{y}_i = K_i \, \bigl(x_{\mathrm{des},i} - x_i\bigr), \qquad \text{saturated to a maximum magnitude},
\end{equation}
with per-task gains $K_{\mathrm{tip}} = 3.5$, $K_{\mathrm{RCM}} = 17.0$, and $K_{\mathrm{cone}} = 6.0$ (as configured in \texttt{ROSADoubleRCMController.cs}). The comparatively high RCM gain reflects the priority given to the safety constraint over tracking accuracy.

The stacked system is solved with a damped least-squares (Levenberg--Marquardt-style) pseudoinverse,
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{q} \\ \dot{\lambda} \end{bmatrix}
= J^{T} \left( J J^{T} + \mu^2 I \right)^{-1} \dot{y}, \qquad \mu = 0.055,
\end{equation}
implemented from scratch with a custom Gauss--Jordan linear solver; no external inverse-kinematics or optimization library is used. Jacobians are computed by numerical finite differencing rather than symbolically, a deliberate simplicity trade-off that keeps the Denavit--Hartenberg table easily replaceable at the cost of some numerical noise.

A null-space secondary objective is projected into the kernel of $J$ and added on top of the primary solution,
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{q} \\ \dot{\lambda} \end{bmatrix}_{\mathrm{total}}
=
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{q} \\ \dot{\lambda} \end{bmatrix}
+ s \left( I - J^{+}J \right) w,
\end{equation}
with null-space scale $s = 0.55$ and $w$ combining three sub-objectives, each with its own gain: keeping $\lambda$ near a nominal target (gain $0.9$), keeping joints away from their limits (gain $0.08$), and biasing the arm away from the skull surface (gain $1.3$). The damped projector minimizes interference with primary tasks; Task~2 additionally suppresses the q4 null-space component whenever its active-set constraint is engaged.

Finally, both joint and $\lambda$ velocities are saturated ($\dot{q}_{\max} = 80^\circ\!/\mathrm{s}$, $\dot{\lambda}_{\max} = 0.55\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$) before Euler integration:
\begin{equation}
q_{k+1} = \mathrm{clamp}(q_k + \dot{q}\,\Delta t), \qquad
\lambda_{k+1} = \mathrm{clamp}(\lambda_k + \dot\lambda\,\Delta t,\; 0.02,\; 0.995).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Task 2 Failure Analysis and Controller Fix}

In the original Task~2 implementation, the tip-target and moving entry-cone tasks were solved as one weighted stack. Near a rank-deficient two-point configuration, the solver could select a wrist branch that drove joint~4 toward its $-170^\circ$ limit. Once the physical clamp became active, the saturated joint command no longer represented the Cartesian direction returned by the inverse-kinematics solve. The result was drift of the tip away from its fixed target even though the requested pose remained reachable. Increasing gains did not address the cause and could amplify the instability.

The corrected controller uses the following compact sequence:
\begin{enumerate}
\item solve tip tracking as the primary Task~2 objective;
\item project entry-cone tracking into the damped null space of the tip Jacobian;
\item when joint~4 enters a $25^\circ$ soft margin and its command points farther outward, remove its Jacobian column, set $\dot q_4=0$, and redistribute the Cartesian command over the remaining joints and $\lambda$;
\item if the unsaturated solution would exceed the joint-speed limit, repeat the solve with increased damping;
\item split long Unity frames into integration substeps no larger than $8$\,ms.
\end{enumerate}
The active set is directional: joint~4 is free to move inward and is constrained only when it would move farther toward the physical limit. Null-space motion on joint~4 is also suppressed while this constraint is active. In the updated run, the Task~2 active set was used in 206 of 1066 logged samples; joint~4 remained between $-145.1^\circ$ and $-28.5^\circ$, while the tip-target error remained below 0.15\,mm. The final implementation retains only these functional mechanisms; temporary A/B switches and Jacobian diagnostics used during investigation were removed.

\subsection{Validation Checks}

Nine pass/fail checks are computed with a 1\,s settling exclusion after each relevant task or phase transition. The thresholds are: entry-RCM stability $\leq 2$\,mm, tip-target tracking $\leq 3$\,mm, entry-/tip-cone tracking $\leq 50$\,mm, and skull/corridor violation $\leq 0$\,mm. Raw phase maxima are still reported in the plots and discussion; therefore they can be higher than the settled values used by the automatic checks.

\section{Limitations}
% =====================================================================

\begin{itemize}
\item The manipulator geometry is ROSA-\emph{like}: a plausible Denavit--Hartenberg table, not measured from an official CAD model.
\item The needle is modeled as a rigid cylinder; there is no tissue interaction or deformation.
\item The controller is purely kinematic: there is no dynamics or force control.
\item Jacobians are computed numerically (finite differencing) rather than in closed form, trading some numerical accuracy for implementation simplicity and DH-table flexibility.
\item Quantitative conclusions rest on a single logged run, not on repeated randomized trials. Passing all checks demonstrates this execution, not a statistical success rate over the complete workspace.
\item The q4 active set is a velocity-level constraint rather than a predictive trajectory optimizer; future configurations may require active constraints on additional joints.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

This project implements a double-RCM task-priority controller for a simulated ROSA-like surgical robot, covering four task modes and a three-phase clinically motivated insertion sequence entirely from scratch (custom damped-least-squares solve, custom null-space projection, and no external IK library). The updated 3361-sample run passes all nine automatic checks using 2\,mm entry-RCM and 3\,mm tip-target thresholds, while recording zero skull/corridor violation. In particular, Task~2 holds the tip within 0.149\,mm despite its moving cone objective, confirming that the strict hierarchy and q4 active set remove the previously observed wrist-limit drift. Task~4 also passes after settling, with 1.727\,mm maximum entry-RCM error. These results validate the implemented kinematic behavior for this run; they do not constitute clinical validation of the ROSA platform or a statistical workspace-wide guarantee.

\subsection*{Future Work}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Replace the finite-difference Jacobian with an analytic one for speed and numerical robustness.
\item Re-introduce anatomical constraints as an explicitly separate validation layer, rather than mixing them back into the core RCM logic.
\item Adopt real ROSA kinematic parameters, if they become accessible.
\item Run randomized multi-trial validation with matched task durations and repeated initial configurations, including configurations near every joint limit.
\item Generalize the q4 active set to a compact multi-joint constrained solver if broader workspace tests expose saturation of other joints.
\end{enumerate}
